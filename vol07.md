### Description

Once, more than five hundred households shared these lanes; now wild grass grows where autumn leaves come and go.

A quiet photographic meditation set in Gijachon and Umulgol, beneath Bukhansan in Seoul. This is not a guide to Korea. It is a way of feeling it.

Through HongSahn's lens, two timelines flow side by side. Leaves arrive and fall along a built walking path; a few ginkgo trees still stand firm where houses once stood. Flowers come, leaves turn, and a peaceful emptiness settles over the slope.

Built between 1969 and 1974, Gijachon was Korea's first residential community for journalists — more than 500 households in its narrow lanes. Today a new town has risen around it, and the old village lingers at the edge, half-remembered, as autumn keeps passing through.

The Korean Photo Essay Series (KPE Archive) is a long-form photographic archive documenting emotional time across Korean landscapes. Season I: Coming and Going Time comprises 30 individual photo essays and 10 thematic collections, 2026.

“This is not a guide to Korea. It is a way of feeling it.”

This volume returns to Gijachon, where the seasons go on coming and going over a vanishing village.
